# VocabStream advanced vocabulary semantic review

This review read all 1,000 entries in `word-advanced` lessons 1–100. For every entry, the English headword, definition, example and Japanese meaning were checked for sense alignment, part of speech, naturalness and obvious factual errors. The reading covered six contiguous groups: lessons 1–15, 16–30, 31–45, 46–65, 66–85 and 86–100. This is an editorial pass, not independent expert certification or individual-word CEFR calibration.

## Corrections

The complete read produced 42 selected entry corrections across 35 lesson files: 23 definitions, 20 examples, 7 Japanese meanings, 20 synonym fields and 23 antonym fields (93 field changes). Related synonyms and antonyms were corrected or cleared where they contradicted the selected sense. Empty synonym/antonym fields deliberately mean that no suitable equivalent was supplied.

Including the earlier two noun-example repairs (`vulnerability`, `accountability`) and ten conservative `duplicateOf` annotations, this change set modifies 54 advanced entries across 44 files: 23 definitions, 22 examples, 7 Japanese meanings, 20 synonym fields, 23 antonym fields and 10 duplicate references.

Every original lesson ID, word string, word position, lesson membership and course level is preserved. No image or sentence-practice metadata was changed by this review.

## Repetition and meaning

The original 1,000 entries contain 841 distinct normalized headwords, or 159 repeated headword occurrences. Forty-five occurrences repeat the same normalized headword and definition. These counts describe repetition, not 159 erroneous entries: different meanings and parts of speech can be legitimate teaching targets. Repetition remains in place to preserve existing learning records.

Repeated same-sense entries include `inference`, `resilience`, `innovation` and `impact`. Ten especially clear duplicate senses already have conservative `duplicateOf` references; this metadata does not remove entries or merge learning history. Other paraphrased overlaps require a separate curriculum decision. Valid distinctions such as `equity` (fairness versus ownership), `reform` (noun versus verb), and `performance` (machine operation versus artistic presentation) remain.

## Remaining limitations

- Later lessons often use generic examples. Before these corrections, 550 of the 600 definitions in lessons 41–100 had at most five words, and 579 examples had at most seven words. By comparison, the first 400 entries had 103 and 155 respectively. These are descriptive indicators of thin context, not evidence that short definitions are wrong. Strong concise definitions were retained.
- Some examples express broad claims about personality, productivity or social outcomes. More concrete individual situations would teach usage better; this review does not certify every illustrative claim as an empirical fact.
- The course mixes relatively common words (`decide`, `simple`, `online`, `explain`) with more specialized vocabulary. Its existing level is a course label, not an independently validated CEFR grade for every entry. No words were moved to another level.
- All four main fields were read for every entry. Synonyms, antonyms and word forms were additionally checked for the selected corrections; this does not claim an exhaustive independent thesaurus or morphology review of all 1,000 entries.
- A later curriculum edition could replace weak generic examples and explicitly distinguish additional repeated senses, but must preserve or migrate progress identities intentionally.

## Reference checks

Definitions were independently phrased. Selected factual and lexical distinctions were checked against primary references: central versus regional government in [Cambridge’s federal entry](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/federal); information supporting a claim in [Cambridge’s evidence entry](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/evidence); the long-term nature of climate in [NOAA’s weather and climate explanation](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/weather_climate.html); nuclear energy and atomic nuclei in [the IAEA explanation](https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/news/what-is-nuclear-energy-the-science-of-nuclear-power); and general price-level increases in [the IMF explanation](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781557758750/ch004.xml). Cambridge’s direct page fetch returned 403, so its available search-index excerpts supplied those two checks.
